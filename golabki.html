Stuffed Cabbage Rolls

Gołąbki

Prep: 45 min Cook: 1.5 hrs Serves 4

Method

1

Prepare the cabbage leaves

Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cut around the core of a large white cabbage to loosen the leaves. Submerge the head and cook for 3–4 minutes. As the outer leaves soften, peel them off one by one with tongs. You need 12–14 large, pliable leaves. Reserve any small or torn inner leaves.

2

Par-cook the rice and onion

Cook 150 g long-grain rice in salted water for 8 minutes — slightly undercooked. Drain and rinse with cold water. Meanwhile, sauté 1 diced onion in oil until golden, about 6 minutes. Add 2 minced garlic cloves and cook 1 more minute. Let cool.

3

Make the filling

In a large bowl, combine 500 g ground pork, the par-cooked rice, sautéed onion and garlic, 1 egg, 1 tsp dried marjoram, salt, and pepper. Mix well with your hands until fully combined. The mixture should be firm and hold its shape.

4

Roll the gołąbki

Trim the thick central rib from each cabbage leaf with a knife to make rolling easier. Place 2–3 tablespoons of filling near the base of each leaf. Fold the sides inward, then roll up tightly from the base, like a burrito. Repeat with all the filling.

5

Layer in the pot

Line the bottom of a large heavy pot with the reserved torn or small cabbage leaves — this prevents the rolls from sticking and burning. Arrange the gołąbki seam-side down in tight layers on top.

6

Braise in tomato sauce

Whisk together 400 g canned crushed tomatoes, 300 ml beef stock, 2 tbsp tomato paste, 1 tsp sugar, salt, and pepper. Pour over the gołąbki — it should come halfway up the rolls. Tuck in 2 bay leaves and 4 allspice berries. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce heat to very low, cover tightly, and braise for 90 minutes.

7

Finish the sauce

Carefully remove the gołąbki with a slotted spoon. Taste the sauce and adjust seasoning — add more salt, pepper, or a pinch of sugar to balance. Remove the bay leaves and allspice. Spoon sauce generously over the rolls.

8

Serve

Serve 2–3 gołąbki per person, topped with plenty of tomato sauce and a dollop of sour cream. Garnish with fresh dill or parsley. Mashed potatoes on the side are traditional.

Chef's Notes

  • Freezer trick: Freeze the whole cabbage overnight and thaw before use — leaves separate perfectly and are already pliable, no blanching needed.
  • Don't fully cook the rice — it finishes cooking inside the rolls, absorbing all the meat juices, which makes the filling deeply flavorful.
  • Roll firmly — loose rolls tend to fall apart in the pot. Tight rolling is important.
  • Tastes better the next day — reheat gently in the sauce; the rolls absorb even more flavor overnight.
Serves 4

Ingredients

  • 1 large head white cabbage
  • 500 g ground pork (or half pork, half beef)
  • 150 g long-grain rice, uncooked
  • 1 large onion, finely diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tsp dried marjoram
  • Salt & black pepper, to taste
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 400 g canned crushed tomatoes
  • 300 ml beef or vegetable stock
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 4 whole allspice berries
  • Sour cream & fresh dill, to serve
